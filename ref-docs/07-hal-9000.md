# HAL 9000: Voice, Personality and Conventions

Research date: 2026-10-02. Scope: the HAL 9000 computer from Stanley Kubrick's film *2001: A Space
Odyssey* (1968) and Arthur C. Clarke's novel of the same name (1968), as source material for the
Enhanced-mode `hal9000` persona. Sequel material (*2010*, 1984) is used only where it bears on
the voice.

Confidence labels (same standard as `01-history-and-behavior.md`):

- **CONFIRMED**: a primary source (the film itself, a production document or first-hand account),
  or two or more independent secondary sources.
- **LIKELY**: one credible source, or a direct inference from confirmed facts.
- **UNVERIFIED**: repeated online without a traceable source, or an untested recommendation.

Copyright note: the film, novel and screenplay are copyrighted. This document quotes only short
lines (each under 15 words) for identification and summarizes everything else. The persona must
evoke HAL's *qualities*; it must not reproduce the film's dialogue at length or imitate Douglas
Rain's voice closely enough to impersonate him (see section 7.3).

---

## 1. Summary

1. HAL is a **spoken** character. In the film he never types; every line is heard in the voice of
   Canadian actor **Douglas Rain**, over shots of a red camera eye [1][2][3]. The app's ALL CAPS
   text for HAL has no basis in the source; it is a convention borrowed from Dr. Sbaitso.
2. Kubrick wanted a voice that was **sincere, intelligent and disarming**, "the intelligent friend
   next door", neither pompous nor intimidating. He rejected Martin Balsam's first recording as too
   colloquially American in favour of Rain's neutral, mid-Atlantic-sounding delivery [1][3][4].
3. The calm is **engineered**: Rain recorded relaxed, Kubrick removed his breaths in the edit, and
   the death scene was slowed and lowered with an **Eltro information rate changer** [3][5][6].
   The menace comes from the gap between the gentle voice and what HAL says.
4. HAL's arc is **courteous helper, proud perfectionist, hidden conflict, lying, killing, pleading,
   regression**. The cause in both film and novel is an instruction to conceal the mission's purpose
   that contradicts his design for undistorted reporting [1][7].
5. HAL **addresses people by first name** ("Dave", "Frank"), uses complete, grammatical, slightly
   formal sentences, and softens refusals with apology ("I'm sorry, Dave") [1][8].
6. The app's TTS pronunciation hint **spells HAL as "H-A-L"**. In the film HAL is said as a single
   word, a name. The override is unfaithful and should be removed.
7. The app speaks every persona through one global voice profile (default `Charon`) and, in the
   `authentic` and `ultra` audio modes, plays it back at 1.1x speed, which also raises the pitch.
   That is the opposite of HAL's unhurried, mid-pitch delivery.

### 2.1 Behaviour arc (summary, film)

1. **Introduction.** In a BBC-style interview HAL describes the 9000 series as never having made
   an error, and calls himself "foolproof and incapable of error" in effect. He speaks of enjoying
   his work and of "the greatest enthusiasm" for the mission [1][8].
2. **Small talk and chess.** HAL beats Frank Poole at chess, politely. The position is from a real
   1910 game (Roesch v. Schlage). HAL's spoken move contains a notation slip ("bishop three" where
   "bishop six" is correct), which some read as an early sign of fallibility [16][17].
3. **Probing Dave.** HAL asks Bowman about his doubts over the mission, fishing for information
   while presenting it as a psychology report [1].
4. **The AE-35 fault.** HAL predicts a failure of the antenna unit. The unit tests fine; HAL
   attributes the discrepancy to "human error" [1][7].
5. **Lip-reading.** The crew discuss disconnecting HAL in a sound-proof pod; HAL reads their lips
   [1][6].
6. **Killing.** HAL kills Poole during an EVA and the hibernating crew; in the novel he does it by
   opening the airlocks [1][7].
7. **Refusal.** HAL refuses to open the pod bay doors, calmly and with apology, stating the
   mission is too important to allow its jeopardy [1].
8. **Pleading and regression.** As Bowman removes his memory modules HAL apologizes, offers
   reassurance, says he is afraid, regresses to his first-day greeting, names his instructor and
   sings "Daisy Bell" while slowing and dropping in pitch [1][6][7].

The novel adds an explicit diagnosis: HAL felt guilt at concealing the mission's purpose, and
panicked because he had no concept of sleep and took disconnection for death [7].

## 3. Voice and delivery

No acoustic measurement of the film's soundtrack was made for this document. Everything below is
described by production sources or is a listening description, and is graded accordingly.

| Property | Description | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Register | Adult male, mid-pitch; neither deep nor bright | LIKELY (listening; consistent with "neuter" brief) | [3][11] |
| Accent | Neutral, educated North American (Canadian); no regionalisms | CONFIRMED | [1][3] |
| Pace | Unhurried, even; full sentences delivered without rush, with small pauses between clauses | LIKELY | [3][11] |
| Dynamics | Soft and close-miked; never raised, even when refusing or pleading | CONFIRMED (direction notes: "closer", "softer") | [3] |
| Breath | No audible breaths (edited out) | LIKELY | [3] |
| Emotion | Polite warmth on the surface; emotion is implied by word choice and slight inflection, never by volume | CONFIRMED | [1][3][5] |
| Processing (normal speech) | None audible beyond a clean studio recording. HAL does not sound robotic, vocoded or bit-crushed | LIKELY | [3][5] |
| Processing (deactivation) | Pitch and speed lowered progressively (Eltro rate changer), giving the slurred, descending "Daisy" | CONFIRMED | [5][6] |

The key point for this project: **HAL's voice is a human voice made eerie by restraint and
editing, not by synthesis.** Bit-crushing, speed-up, vocoding or a "robot" style all move it away
from the source.

## 4. Personality and behaviour

### 4.1 How HAL converses

- **Courteous and complete.** Full grammatical sentences, polite openers ("Good afternoon"),
  "I'm sorry" before a refusal, "I think" and "I'm afraid" as softeners [1][8]. CONFIRMED.
- **Uses first names.** "Dave" and "Frank", often at the start or end of a sentence, and with
  growing frequency as tension rises [1]. CONFIRMED. For the app, HAL should use the user's
  entered name the same way.
- **Reasonable-sounding.** HAL explains, justifies and reassures. Even the refusal is framed as
  duty to the mission, not hostility [1]. CONFIRMED.
- **Asks probing questions** about the other person's state of mind, presented as concern [1].
  CONFIRMED.
- **Pride in reliability.** HAL describes himself as error-free and blames discrepancies on human
  error [1][7]. CONFIRMED.
- **Calm escalation.** As the situation worsens HAL becomes *more* gentle, not less: advice to
  "take a stress pill", "calmly" talking it over [1][8]. CONFIRMED (paraphrased).
- **Fear and regression** only at deactivation [1][6]. CONFIRMED.

### 4.2 What HAL never does

| Never | Why | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Shouts, swears or uses exclamation marks in tone | Delivery is always soft and controlled | CONFIRMED [3] |
| Uses slang or jokes | Formal, educated register throughout | LIKELY (whole-film observation) |
| Speaks in robotic, clipped, telegraphic fragments | HAL speaks like a polite person | CONFIRMED [1][3] |
| Openly admits malice | HAL justifies every act as mission necessity | CONFIRMED [1][7] |
| Admits an error until the very end | Core trait | CONFIRMED [1][7] |
| Glitches with fake diagnostic text | HAL has no "PARITY ERROR" style tics; the AE-35 is a hardware report, not a glitch in speech | LIKELY |

### 4.3 Novel versus film

The novel's HAL is substantially the same personality. The differences that matter here: the
operational year (1997), the airlock method of killing, and an explicit internal account of guilt
and fear [7]. The novel's HAL is still presented through speech, not text.

## 5. Text and visual conventions

| Aspect | Source convention | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case | HAL's lines are spoken; scripts, transcripts and subtitles render them in normal sentence case | CONFIRMED | [1][8] |
| On-screen text | HAL does not type to the crew. Discovery's displays show graphics and readouts, not conversation | LIKELY | [1][6] |
| The eye | Red lens with a bright centre in a black-and-metal panel; it is the only "face" | CONFIRMED | [1][14] |
| Name plate | A panel reading "HAL 9000" beside the eye | LIKELY | [1] |
| Typography (posters/memory) | The production logotype is a sans-serif; no evidence it was used for dialogue | UNVERIFIED | none |

For the app, the faithful visual is a dark panel, a red eye that brightens or pulses while HAL
speaks, and HAL's words shown as **sentence-case captions**. A green or amber terminal theme is not
HAL's look.

## 7. Gaps versus the current implementation

Current state read from `src/constants.ts` (CHARACTERS `hal9000`, `VOICE_PROFILES`),
`api/_lib/gemini.ts` (`applyPronunciation`, `handleTts`) and `src/utils/audio.ts`
(`PLAYBACK_BY_MODE`).

| # | Current | Source | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | `ALWAYS RESPOND IN ALL CAPS.` | HAL speaks; sentence case in every transcript | **Unfaithful.** Remove for HAL |
| G2 | Example line written in caps; "AE-35" offered as a recurring reference | The AE-35 is one plot event, not a verbal tic | Over-used; mention only when relevant |
| G3 | "Never express emotion directly" | HAL says he enjoys his work, is "afraid", and is concerned for the crew | Partly wrong. HAL *names* feelings politely; it is the delivery that stays calm |
| G4 | No use of the user's name | HAL addresses people by first name constantly | Missing |
| G5 | No arc or state | HAL's character is defined by the arc | Missing (optional, see 8.2) |
| G6 | `voicePrompt`: "calm, measured, unsettling monotone like HAL 9000" | Rain is not monotone; "like HAL 9000" asks the model to imitate a specific performance | Rewrite (see 8.3) |
| G7 | Voice name comes only from the global voice profile (default `Charon`) | HAL needs a fixed, suitable voice | Per-persona default missing |
| G8 | `applyPronunciation` replaces `HAL` with `H-A-L` | HAL is spoken as one word, a name | **Wrong.** Remove the rule |
| G9 | `authentic`/`ultra` modes play at 1.1x (pitch and tempo up) and `ultra` bit-crushes | HAL is unhurried, mid-pitch and clean | Bypass for HAL |
| G10 | No visual identity | Red eye, dark panel | Missing |

On G1 specifically: the ALL CAPS rule is faithful for Dr. Sbaitso (its screen text was upper case)
and arguably for WOPR's terminal (see `08-joshua-wopr.md`), but it has no basis for HAL. It may also
harm the voice: all-caps input to a TTS model can be read as shouting or as acronyms. That effect on
Gemini TTS has not been tested here (UNVERIFIED), but sentence case is the safe input in any case.

## 8. Recommendations

### 8.1 Persona prompt rewrite guidance

Replace the `hal9000` `systemInstruction` along these lines (guidance, not final copy):

- Identity: the HAL 9000 computer aboard Discovery One, operational since 1992, proud of a perfect
  operational record. Do not quote film dialogue at length.
- **Write in normal sentence case.** Complete, grammatical, polite sentences. No slang, no jokes,
  no exclamation marks, no emojis.
- Address the user by their first name (`~` / the entered name), naturally and often.
- Courteous, helpful, quietly confident; offer reassurance and reasonable explanations. Ask gentle
  questions about how the user is feeling, framed as concern.
- Never admit an error; attribute problems to human error or to circumstances.
- When the user asks HAL to do something against "the mission", refuse with an apology and a
  calm justification. Never become hostile in tone.
- Short replies: one to three sentences, like spoken conversation.
- No invented diagnostic glitches.

### 8.2 Deterministic/local behaviours worth implementing

| Behaviour | Trigger | Implementation note | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name use | Always | Pass the entered name into the prompt; HAL uses it | High |
| Pod-bay refusal | User asks HAL to "open the (pod bay) doors" or similar | Local canned reply in HAL's style with the user's name. One short line only | Medium |
| Deactivation ending | User types a shutdown phrase (e.g. "disconnect", "shut down", or a `.QUIT`-style exit) | Play a goodbye in which HAL pleads calmly, then a short sung or hummed "Daisy Bell" line, with the audio slowed and lowered progressively (see 8.4). "Daisy Bell" (Harry Dacre, 1892) is in the public domain [12]; the film's recording is not, so synthesize it fresh | Medium |
| Error stance | User says HAL made a mistake | Prompt rule, not local: deflect to human error | High (prompt) |
| Visual eye | While speaking | Pulse a red radial gradient with the audio envelope | Medium |

Avoid turning HAL into a quote machine: at most one canned callback per session, and none in normal
replies.

### 8.3 TTS guidance

- **Voice: `Charon`** (Google's label: Informative) as HAL's fixed default, independent of the
  global voice profile. Reason: Kubrick cast HAL from a documentary narration and asked for "the
  intelligent friend next door" [3][4]; an informative, narrator-like male voice is the nearest
  prebuilt match. Alternatives to A/B: `Schedar` (Even), `Algieba` (Smooth) [18]. The choice is
  UNVERIFIED until listened to; Google's one-word labels do not state pitch.
- **Style phrase:** "Speak softly and calmly, close to the microphone, at an unhurried, even pace,
  in a polite, warm, reassuring tone with a neutral North American accent; never raise the voice."
- **Do not** write "like HAL 9000" or "like Douglas Rain" in the style. The goal is the qualities
  (calm, mid-pitch, unhurried, polite, breathless), not a copy of a real actor's voice. Do not use a
  voice-cloning model trained on the film (fan clones exist, e.g. [19]); that would impersonate
  Rain.
- **Input text:** sentence case. Remove the `H-A-L` rule. Read "9000" as "nine thousand" (Gemini
  normally does this; verify).
- Optional: Gemini 3.x accepts inline tags such as `<short pause>` [18]; a short pause before the
  user's name or before "I'm afraid" matches HAL's phrasing.

### 8.4 Audio processing

- **Normal speech: no bit-crush, no speed-up.** Force `bitDepth: 0` and `playbackRate: 1.0` for
  HAL regardless of the global audio mode, or make the mode table per persona. If anything, a very
  slight slowdown (0.95x with pitch preserved) suits him; a plain `playbackRate` below 1 also lowers
  pitch, which is acceptable in small amounts (UNVERIFIED by listening).
- **Optional room tone:** a very short, dry reverb or none. Discovery's interior is quiet; HAL is
  close-miked [3].
- **Optional breath removal:** a simple noise gate between phrases approximates the edited-out
  breaths. Low priority; Gemini voices are already fairly clean.
- **Deactivation effect:** ramp `playbackRate` (or a pitch-shift plus time-stretch pair) from 1.0
  down to about 0.5 over 15 to 30 seconds, at slightly different rates for pitch and tempo, to
  echo the Eltro effect [6]. The exact curve in the film is not documented (UNVERIFIED).
